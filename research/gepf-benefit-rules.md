# GEPF benefit rules and current status (research note)

Research date: 2026-09-09. Compiled for the SA retirement-planning tool.

Access note: gepf.co.za, gpaa.gov.za, dpsa.gov.za, pmg.org.za, saflii.org and all major SA press domains were blocked by the network egress proxy during this session, so every fact below was extracted from search-engine snippets of the cited pages rather than a full fetch. Where a snippet was ambiguous the confidence is marked medium or low, and the item is listed under open questions. All source URLs are primary/official where possible.

## 1. Narrative summary

**Retirement benefit (10 or more years' pensionable service).** The GEPF is a defined-benefit fund governed by the Government Employees Pension Law 21 of 1996 (GEP Law) and its Rules. At normal retirement (age 60) a member with at least 10 years' pensionable service receives a once-off gratuity of 6.72% x final salary x years of pensionable service, plus a life annuity of (1/55 x final salary x years of pensionable service) + R360 per year. "Final salary" is the average pensionable salary over the last 24 months of service (and not less than the salary at 30 April 1997). Pensionable service includes purchased ("bought-back") service and excludes leave without pay; for Services members (SAPS, SANDF, Correctional Services, intelligence) service beyond 10 years is uplifted by one quarter. These formula constants (6.72%, 1/55, R360) have been unchanged since the Fund's inception in 1996.

**Under 10 years.** A member retiring with fewer than 10 years receives a gratuity only, equal to their actuarial interest (no annuity). Because actuarial interest depends on the revised factors, such members were affected by the October 2025 factor change.

**Early retirement (55 to 59).** Benefits are calculated on the normal formula and then reduced by one third of one percent for each month between the early retirement date and the member's 60th birthday (about 4% per year; roughly 19.9% at exactly 55). No reduction applies where the employer initiates early retirement under section 16(6) of the Public Service Act and agrees to pay the penalty (the 2019 and 2025 PSCBC "early retirement without penalisation" programmes), or on ill-health, injury-on-duty or restructuring/abolition-of-post discharges, which attract enhanced (unreduced and service-enhanced) benefits.

**Normal retirement age and working to 65.** The GEPF normal retirement age is 60. Under the Public Service Act an officer has the right to retire from 55 and must retire at 65 (retention beyond 65 is possible for up to two years with executive-authority approval). Retiring at any point between 60 and 65 attracts no reduction; service and final salary simply keep accruing.

**Resignation benefit = actuarial interest (all members).** Since 1 April 2012 every resigning member (regardless of length of service) receives their actuarial interest. Operationally it is computed as pensionable service x final salary x an age-dependent factor F(Z) set by the Board on the actuary's advice after consultation with the Minister of Finance and employee organisations (Rules 14.4.1/14.4.2). The GEPF FAQ example shows factors of 0.2036 at age 40 and 0.20431 at age 41 (10 years' service, R300 000 salary gives R610 800). Rule 14.4.1(a) also contains a floor formula N(adj) x FS x F(Z) x [1 + 0.04 x (60 - Z)], where Z is the member's pension retirement age, so the bracket uplifts members whose retirement age is below 60.

**Revised factors from 1 October 2025.** Following the 31 March 2024 statutory valuation, the GEPF adopted new actuarial interest factors effective 1 October 2025 (notice dated 1 September 2025; consultation with unions completed 31 July 2025). The new factors produce actuarial interest values on average 15% lower than the 2021 factors (which themselves were about 14% lower than the 2018 factors when implemented on 1 November 2022). Affected: all resignations, any exit with under 10 years' service, and savings-component (two-pot) withdrawals for members under 55. Not affected: pensioners, and retirement benefits for members with 10+ years. Members whose exit date fell before 1 October 2025 are calculated on the 2021 factors even if paid later. The full factor table (Appendix 8 of the 2024 valuation report) could not be retrieved; see open questions. **Observed data point (added 12 Sep 2026):** a GEPF "Estimate of Benefits" dated 31 March 2026 — the first seen on the revised basis — gives a resignation benefit of R7 358 306.34 on 26 years' pensionable service and a final average salary of R1 146 998.90, implying F = 0.2467 for a member aged 64.25. Against the 2021 curve anchored to the same member's 31 May 2025 statement (F = 0.2629 at 63.42) that is a reduction of 7.0%, not 15%. The 15% figure is GEPF's average across all ages; the two data points together imply the cut is much smaller near retirement and correspondingly larger for younger members, which is the pattern a discount-rate change produces. `src/data/gepfRules.ts` now fits the 2025 curve to both facts.

**Options on resignation.** Cash lump sum (taxed on the SARS withdrawal table: first R27 500 tax-free, then 18%, 27% and 36% bands, unchanged since the 2024 tax year), or tax-free transfer to a pension preservation fund, retirement annuity or new employer's fund. Under two-pot (from 1 September 2024) the vested and savings components are payable on resignation (cash or transfer); the retirement component must be preserved, either in the GEPF as a deferred pension (calculated on the resignation-date salary and increased with pensioner increases) or transferred to the retirement component of another approved fund, which includes a preservation fund.

**Spouse, guarantee, funeral, children.** The default spouse's pension is 50% of the member's pension; the member may irrevocably elect 75% at retirement, paid for by reducing the gratuity or the annuity by a percentage that depends on the ages and genders of member and spouse. Retirement annuities are guaranteed for five years: on death within five years the balance of the five years' payments is paid as a lump sum. Funeral benefit: R15 000 for the member/pensioner or spouse, R6 000 for an eligible child. Child pensions are paid to children under 22 (for life if disabled); with a surviving spouse the remaining 50% is split among children (one child gets 25%), without a spouse each child gets 25% (equal split if more than four; minimum 10%).

**Post-retirement medical subsidy (the big cost of resigning).** Retirees with 15 or more years' service (10 for ill-health) who were on a medical scheme for at least 12 months before retirement receive a lifelong employer subsidy of 75% of their medical-scheme contribution, capped. The 2025 DPSA determination caps the subsidy at R1 918 per month for a principal member without dependants and R3 836 with dependants (from 1 January 2025); the 2026 determination raises the single-member cap to R2 004 (1 January 2026) and R2 014 (1 April 2026), i.e. 4.5% plus 0.5%, implying about R4 009 to R4 029 for member-plus-dependants. Resignation forfeits this benefit entirely (and the GEPF funeral benefit), which for a retiree can be worth R23 000 to R48 000 a year for life.

**Pension increases.** The GEP Law requires an annual increase on 1 April of at least 75% of the year-on-year CPI to the previous 30 November, plus any catch-up needed to keep the pension at 75% of its original purchasing power; increases above that are at the Board's discretion, subject to affordability, and the Board's practice is to grant 100% of CPI where affordable. Increases: April 2019 5.2%; 2020 3.6%; 2021 3.2%; 2022 5.5%; 2023 5.55% (75% of 7.4% CPI); 2024 6.0% (109% of 5.5% CPI); 2025 2.9% (100%); 2026 3.5% (100% of November 2025 CPI).

**Funding, assets, membership, allocation.** The 31 March 2024 statutory valuation showed a funding level of 119% before contingency reserves (110.1% at the 2021 valuation), on 1 264 000 active members and 553 049 pensioners/beneficiaries. The 2024/25 annual report shows assets of R2.69 trillion at 31 March 2025 (14.1% investment return; 13.1% portfolio growth), 1 267 539 active members and 565 221 pensioners and beneficiaries; the Fund told the NCOP Select Committee on Finance on 26 May 2026 that it had passed R3 trillion during 2025/26 (unaudited). Asset mix at 31 March 2025 (annual report): listed equities R1 629.6bn (about 61%), bills and bonds R830.4bn (about 31%), unlisted equities R108.5bn (about 4%), collective investment schemes R25bn; the Fund described the mix to Parliament as roughly 50% equities, 40% bonds, remainder offshore/property. Offshore holdings were R319bn (13.7%) at March 2024 against a 15% limit agreed with the Minister of Finance in 2021 (10% global plus 5% rest of Africa). Isibaya/unlisted developmental investments are about R88bn invested (R94bn committed), roughly 3.3% of the fund, with a 4.25% IRR since 2006.

**Regulation 28 and the state guarantee.** The GEPF is not subject to the Pension Funds Act, so Regulation 28 does not apply; its investment limits come from its own Investment Policy Statement agreed with the Minister of Finance. Benefits are defined in the GEP Law and are guaranteed: if the Fund cannot meet them, government (the taxpayer) must fund the shortfall; the Law requires a minimum 90% funding level, below which a contingent liability for the state arises. Contributions are 7.5% (member) and 13% (employer; 16% for Services members).

**Two-pot statistics.** From 1 September 2024 to 28 February 2025 the GEPF received just over 507 250 savings-withdrawal applications totalling R13.6bn; the 2024/25 annual report records 691 501 applications received and 564 547 paid, amounting to R14.37bn, by 31 March 2025. The average early claim was about R27 500. Withdrawals were suspended 7 to 21 October 2025 to load the new factors, closed on 20 February 2026 and reopened on 2 March 2026 for the 2026/27 tax year. No official 2025/26 full-year total was found.

**Recent news relevant to would-be resigners.** The October 2025 factor change triggered widespread social-media claims that money had "disappeared", which the GEPF, PSA and SAnews rebutted; there is no published evidence of a resignation surge (resignation claimants were 22 671 in 2023/24 and 22 452 in 2024/25). The GEPF Rules require payment within 60 days of the benefit becoming payable (with all documents in), with interest on late payments; the GPAA's internal target has been 45 days, but the two-pot volume caused backlogs in normal exit processing in 2024/25. In 2026 the PIC's governance came under renewed scrutiny (Isibaya portfolio questions in June, CEO suspension in July, PSA calling for a review of the investment mandate), and the GEPF issued a statement reassuring members of its financial stability.

## 3. Open questions

1. The actual F(Z) factor table by age (2021 basis and the 1 October 2025 basis) could not be retrieved; it sits in Appendix 8 of the 31 March 2024 statutory valuation report and in the 1 September 2025 notice/FAQ PDFs, all on gepf.co.za which was blocked. Only two data points are known (0.2036 at 40, 0.20431 at 41, from an FAQ example whose dating suggests the 2021 basis; this is not confirmed). For modelling, a stop-gap is to scale a smooth age curve through those points and apply a 15% haircut for post-October-2025 exits, clearly labelled as an estimate.
2. Exact wording of Rules 14.4.1 and 14.4.2 (how G and A, gratuity and annuity, enter the calculation for older members, and whether Z in [1+0.04(60-Z)] is the pension retirement age or the member's age) needs verification against the GEPF Rules PDF.
3. The 2026 medical-subsidy cap for member-plus-dependants (about R4 009 / R4 029) is derived from the single-member cap and the announced 4.5% and 0.5% adjustments, not read directly from the DPSA 2026 determination.
4. No official GEPF two-pot total for the 2025/26 tax year (1 March 2025 to 28 February 2026) was found; the latest hard numbers are to 31 March 2025.
5. No published evidence of a resignation surge ahead of 1 October 2025 was found; the 2025/26 annual report (expected around November 2026) will show whether resignation claims rose.
6. The precise GEP Law section that obliges the state to fund a shortfall was not confirmed; sources agree on the guarantee in substance and on the 90% minimum funding requirement.
7. Whether the offshore limit has moved beyond 15% since 2021 is unconfirmed (one secondary source mentioned 20%; official sources say 15%).
8. The percentage reduction in annuity for the 75% spouse election is quoted individually by the GEPF (age and gender based); no public table was found.
