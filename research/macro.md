## 4. SARB inflation target — status

- Historically SARB targeted **3–6% CPI** (in place ~25 years).
- **2025 reform**: through 2025 MPC statements, the SARB signalled it now targets the **bottom of the
  range / a 3% point target**. The **July 2025 MPC statement** explicitly said policy would be set "so
  that inflation goes back to 3%", anchoring longer-run expectations at 3% (repo cut 25bp to 7%,
  effective 1 Aug 2025, unanimous decision). This was reaffirmed in the September and November 2025
  statements. The SARB has publicly pushed back on characterising this as "reducing" the target (it says
  the 3–6% range legally remains until National Treasury formally revises the target), but in practice
  the Bank's own forecasting and communication now anchor on a **3% point target, +/- 1 pp**.
- **Practical effect**: 2026 CPI prints (3.1–5.0%) have been running above the 3% anchor for most of the
  year, testing the SARB's credibility on the new framework, and contributing to the repo rate path below.

Sources: [SARB MPC Statement, July 2025](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/publications/publication-detail-pages/statements/monetary-policy-statements/2025/july); [SARB MPC Statement, September 2025](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/publications/publication-detail-pages/statements/monetary-policy-statements/2025/september); [SARB MPC Statement, November 2025](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/publications/publication-detail-pages/statements/monetary-policy-statements/2025/november); [Central Banking — SA's central bank denies reducing inflation target](https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/monetary-policy/7973452/south-africa%E2%80%99s-central-bank-denies-reducing-inflation-target); [Daily Maverick — SARB focus on bottom of target range](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-07-31-sarb-focus-bottom-inflation-target-range-cuts-rates/).

## 6. GDP growth and unemployment

- **GDP growth**: 2024 revised to **+0.5%**; **2025 full year: +1.1%** (strongest since 2022, driven by
  agriculture recovery and finance/real estate/trade); **2026 forecast ≈ +1.2%**, rising toward 1.6% in
  2027 and averaging ~1.8% over 2026–2028 per Treasury, on improved energy supply, mining and reforms.
- **Unemployment (official, narrow)**: Q2 2025 33.2% → Q3 2025 31.9% → Q4 2025 31.4% → **Q1 2026 32.7%**
  (unemployed persons +301,000 to 8.137 million). Youth unemployment remains above 46%. The labour market
  has shown only marginal, uneven improvement despite the growth pickup.

Sources: [Treasury 2026 Budget Review — Economic Outlook (Ch.2)](https://www.treasury.gov.za/documents/National%20Budget/2026/review/Chapter%202.pdf); [Trading Economics — SA unemployment](https://tradingeconomics.com/south-africa/unemployment-rate); [UN — Macroeconomic Trends in SA, Jan 2026](https://southafrica.un.org/en/download/200695/309015).

## 7. Government debt-to-GDP, debt service, ratings, FATF

- **Debt-to-GDP**: revised 2025/26 peak of **77.4%–78.9%** of GDP (upward revision from the 76.2%
  projected in March 2025, due to weaker nominal GDP growth and increased issuance), falling to **77.3%
  in 2026/27** and to **~76.5% by 2028/29**. Finance Minister Godongwana: "for the first time in 17
  years, debt will stabilise and it will continue to fall."
- **Debt-service cost**: remains the fastest-growing line item in the budget (~a fifth of main-budget
  revenue going to interest), though the 2026 Budget projects debt-service costs beginning to fall as a
  share of revenue as the primary surplus widens. (Exact 2025/26 ratio ~21–22% of revenue — consistent
  with the prior estimate; Treasury's Budget Review Chapter 7 has the precise figure.)
- **Credit ratings — 2025/26 actions (first upgrades in ~20 years)**:
  - **S&P Global**: upgraded foreign-currency rating **BB- → BB** in November 2025 (local currency to
    BB+), both with a **positive outlook** — S&P's first SA upgrade in 20 years.
  - **Fitch**: upgraded foreign & local currency ratings **BB- → BB** on 5 June 2026 — Fitch's first SA
    upgrade in almost 21 years, citing the shift to primary fiscal surpluses, improved revenue collection
    and energy/logistics reforms.
  - **Moody's**: placed its Ba2 rating on **positive outlook** following S&P's move (no upgrade yet as of
    Sept 2026).
  - **Net effect**: all three agencies now rate SA at **BB / Ba2** — two notches below investment grade —
    with a positive outlook from two of the three, implying a plausible further upgrade within 12–18
    months if the fiscal trajectory holds.
- **FATF grey list**: South Africa was placed on the grey list in **February 2023** (2021 mutual
  evaluation found deficiencies in 20 of 40 FATF recommendations). It **exited the grey list on 24
  October 2025** after addressing all 22 action-plan items (confirmed complete by June 2025, on-site
  verification in July 2025) — a genuine, dated, sourced event, not an estimate.

Sources: [Treasury — 2026 Budget Review, Government Debt (Ch.7)](https://www.treasury.gov.za/documents/National%20Budget/2026/review/Chapter%207.pdf); [Moonstone — Is SA finally turning the corner on debt?](https://www.moonstone.co.za/is-south-africa-finally-turning-the-corner-on-debt/); [AmCham SA — Budget marks turning point with debt to peak](https://www.amcham.co.za/news/south-africas-budget-marks-turning-point-debt-peak); [Moneyweb — SA ratings upgraded by S&P Global](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/economy/south-africas-ratings-upgraded-by-sp-global/); [gov.za — Government welcomes Fitch ratings upgrade (Jun 2026)](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/government-welcomes-fitch-ratings-upgrade-south-africa-08-jun-2026); [Mondaq — Two upgrades in 12 months](https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/financial-services/1801196/two-upgrades-in-12-months-south-africas-credit-rating-renaissance); [Treasury/FIC — SA exits the FATF greylist, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2025/2025102401%20MEDIA%20STATEMENT-SOUTH%20AFRICA%20EXITS%20THE%20FATF%20GREYLIST%20ON%2024%20OCTOBER%20%202025.pdf).

## 11. GEPF fund status (for cross-reference with gepfRules.ts)

- **31 March 2024 statutory actuarial valuation**: funding level **119.0%** on the best-estimate
  liability basis (fair value of net assets R2.34 trillion vs best-estimate liabilities R1.97 trillion);
  including contingency reserves the funding level is **81.6%** (the Fund can afford only 41% of its
  recommended contingency reserves, up from 21% in 2021).
- **2024/25 financial year**: assets grew to **R2.69 trillion** (some releases cite ~R2.7 trillion),
  investment return **13.1–14.1%** (sources differ slightly by which return measure is quoted).
  Membership: **>1.2 million active members**, **>500,000 pensioners and beneficiaries** — GEPF remains
  the largest pension fund in Africa. Member contributions rose from R91.93bn (2023/24) to R95.63bn
  (2024/25, +4.0%); benefits paid rose from R142.36bn to R167.16bn (+17.6%).

Sources: [GEPF — statutory valuation report 2024](https://gepf.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/GEPF-statutory-valuation-report-2024-Final-signed.pdf); [GEPF — Fund remains financially strong, 13.1% growth 2024/25](https://gepf.co.za/the-gepf-remains-financially-strong-and-resilient-with-13-1-in-investment-portfolio-growth-forthe-year-2024-2025/); [GEPF Annual Report 2024/25](https://gepf.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/GEPF_AR_2025_digital.pdf).
