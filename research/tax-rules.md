# South African personal tax rules relevant to retirement — 2025/26 and 2026/27

**Researched:** 2026-09-09. **As of:** tax year 2026/27 (1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027), with the preceding 2025/26 year (1 March 2025 – 28 February 2026) recorded for comparison.

**Method / caveat.** The sandbox egress proxy blocked direct fetches of sars.gov.za, treasury.gov.za, gepf.co.za and most SA financial press, so every figure below was extracted from search-engine snippets of the primary documents (SARS Budget 2026 Tax Guide, SARS rates pages, Treasury Budget Review 2026, GEPF/GPAA notices) and cross-checked against at least one reputable secondary source (Sanlam, Allan Gray, Moonstone, TaxTim, Werksmans, RSM, Daily Maverick, Business Day). All cumulative "base tax" amounts and all age-threshold figures were also re-derived arithmetically from the marginal rates and rebates and reconcile exactly. Confidence is "high" where a SARS/Treasury-domain snippet showed the number, "medium" where only secondary sources did.

**Additional medical expenses tax credit (s6B)** ([SARS AMTC page](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/additional-medical-expenses-tax-credit/)):
- **65 or older** (or taxpayer/spouse/child with a disability): 33.3 % of medical scheme contributions **exceeding 3 × the MTC**, plus 33.3 % of qualifying out-of-pocket medical expenses. No taxable-income floor.

- The AMTC is non-refundable and can only reduce tax to nil.

## 4. Retirement fund lump sum benefits (retirement / death / severance)

Table unchanged for 2025/26 and 2026/27; effective since 1 March 2023 (previously R500 000 tax-free; tables were last amended in 2014 before that) ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/retirement-lump-sum-benefits/), [Allan Gray](https://www.allangray.co.za/latest-insights/personal-investing/unpacking-the-changes-to-the-retirement-fund-lump-sum-tax-tables/), [TaxTim Budget 2026](https://www.taxtim.com/za/blog/budget-2026)).

| Taxable lump sum (R, cumulative) | Tax |
|---|---|
| 1 – 550 000 | 0 % |
| 550 001 – 770 000 | 18 % of amount above R550 000 |
| 770 001 – 1 155 000 | R39 600 + 27 % of amount above R770 000 |
| 1 155 001 and above | R143 550 + 36 % of amount above R1 155 000 |

**Cumulative aggregation rule** (Second Schedule to the Income Tax Act; [SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/retirement-lump-sum-benefits/), [SA Tax Guide](https://www.sataxguide.co.za/retirement-lump-sum-benefits-tax-tables/)): tax on the current lump sum X = tax per table on [X + all retirement fund lump sum benefits accrued since **1 October 2007** + all withdrawal benefits accrued since **1 March 2009** + all severance benefits accrued since **1 March 2011**] **less** tax per table on the same aggregate excluding X. The notional tax on prior lump sums is recomputed using the current table (not the tax actually paid) ([SARS guide LAPD-IT-G03](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Ops/Guides/LAPD-IT-G03-Guide-on-the-calculation-of-the-tax-payable-on-lump-sum-benefits.pdf)). Result: the R550 000 tax-free band is a once-in-a-lifetime allowance.

Severance benefits (retrenchment) use this same table ([SARS Tax and Retrenchment](https://www.sars.gov.za/individuals/tax-during-all-life-stages-and-events/tax-and-retrenchment/), [TaxTim](https://www.taxtim.com/za/calculators/retrenchment-tax)).

**GEPF-specific:** benefits accrued for service before 1 March 1998 remain tax-free (public sector funds became taxable from 1 March 1998, Income Tax Act 28 of 1997; the exempt portion is computed under SARS Practice Note RF 1/98 "Formula C") and this exemption is in addition to the R550 000 band ([Momentum Legal Update 2/2023](https://sls-fresco.momentum.co.za/files/documents/invest-and-save/legal-updates/legal-update-2-of-2023-public-sector-fund-benefits.pdf), [Pristine Wealth](https://pristinewealth.co.za/gepf-pre-1998-pension-benefit/)).

### Worked example (researcher's own calculation using the SARS formula — SARS's guide contains its own examples that could not be fetched)
Member took a R100 000 withdrawal benefit in 2015 (after 1 March 2009) and now retires in 2026/27 with a R900 000 gratuity.
1. Tax on aggregate R1 000 000 per retirement table = R39 600 + 27 % × (1 000 000 − 770 000) = R39 600 + R62 100 = **R101 700**.
2. Tax on prior aggregate R100 000 per retirement table = **R0**.
3. Tax on the R900 000 = R101 700 − R0 = **R101 700** (effective 11.3 %). Had there been no prior withdrawal, tax on R900 000 would be R39 600 + 27 % × 130 000 = **R74 700**.

## 6. Two-pot retirement system (from 1 September 2024)

Sources: [SARS two-pot page](https://www.sars.gov.za/two-pot-retirement-system/), [SARS media release: tax implications](https://www.sars.gov.za/media-release/tax-implications-of-withdrawing-from-two-pot-retirement-system/), [SARS tax directive enhancements](https://www.sars.gov.za/latest-news/tax-directives-enhancements-and-tax-implications-of-the-two-pot-retirement-system/), [Treasury FAQ Aug 2024](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2024/2024%20Two-pot%20System%20Updated%20%20FAQ%20August%202024.pdf), [GEPF two-pot brochure](https://www.gepf.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Two-Pot-Retirement-System-Brochure-V2-1.pdf).

- **Seeding:** on 31 August 2024, 10 % of the vested component, capped at **R30 000**, was transferred into the savings component as opening balance. (GEPF, as a defined-benefit fund, expresses this via pensionable service; members with a vested benefit above R300 000 got the R30 000 cap.)
- **Contribution split from 1 Sept 2024:** one-third to the savings component, two-thirds to the retirement component (GEPF: one-third / two-thirds of future pensionable service).
- **Savings withdrawal benefit:** **once per tax year**, minimum **R2 000**, up to the full savings-component balance, while still a member. Taxed at the member's **marginal rate** — SARS applies the "annual payment/bonus" PAYE method to the withdrawal, with no retirement tables, exemptions or tax-free amounts. A tax directive is required for every withdrawal; the fund must supply the member's tax number and annual income. Any outstanding SARS debt (assessed tax, provisional tax, admin penalties) is deducted from the payout via an IT88L stop order unless a payment arrangement/suspension is in place. Fund administration fees may also apply.
- **Vested component on resignation:** may still be taken in cash (pre-1 Sept 2024 rights preserved); taxed on the **withdrawal table** (§5). Savings-component cash on resignation is taxed at marginal rates as a savings withdrawal benefit ([Glacier](https://www.glacierinsights.co.za/blog/retirement-insights/two-pot-retirement-system-upon-resignation), [Liberty FAQ](https://www.liberty.co.za/Documents/two-pot-retirement-system-faq.pdf)).
- **Retirement component:** cannot be accessed before retirement and must be **annuitised in full** at retirement (subject to the de minimis rule, §9). At retirement the savings-component balance may be taken in cash and is taxed on the **retirement table** (§4).
- Statistics: by 31 January 2025 SARS had received 2 664 279 directive applications and approved 2 403 379, paying R43.42 billion gross ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/media-release/more-than-2-million-taxpayers-withdraw-from-their-savings-pot/)); cumulative gross withdrawals exceeded R60 billion during 2026, with 62 % of 2026/27-year claims being third withdrawals ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/two-pot-withdrawals-surge-as-repeat-claims-reshape-savings-behaviour/)).
- Illustrative (researcher's calculation): a member with taxable income of R400 000 (2026/27 marginal rate 31 %) withdrawing R30 000 would have roughly R9 300 withheld before any SARS-debt offset.

## 8. Retirement contribution deduction (s11F)

- Deduction = **27.5 %** of the greater of remuneration or taxable income, capped at **R350 000** for 2025/26 (cap unchanged from 1 March 2016) and **R430 000** for 2026/27 (from 1 March 2026). Excess contributions roll forward to later years (s11F(4)) and, if never deducted, are set off against lump sums / annuity income (s10C) ([SARS s11F](https://www.sars.gov.za/latest-news/retirement-fund-contribution-deductions-section-11f2a/), [Sanlam](https://seb-news.sanlam.co.za/consultant-toolkit/key-retirement-fund-values-and-changes-effective-1-march-2026/), [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/changes-to-cgt-tax-free-savings-retirement-contributions-and-donations/)).

## 9. De minimis and the one-third rule

- **One-third rule:** on retirement from a pension, pension preservation, provident, provident preservation or retirement annuity fund, at most **one-third** of the retirement interest may be taken in cash; **two-thirds** must buy an annuity (living or guaranteed). Provident-fund contributions made before 1 March 2021 (and all contributions for members aged 55+ on that date) are "vested" and may still be taken fully in cash ([SARS 1 March 2021 amendments](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Docs/TaxDirectives/1-March-2021-Legislative-Amendments.pdf), [SARS Tax and Retirement](https://www.sars.gov.za/individuals/tax-during-all-life-stages-and-events/tax-and-retirement/)).
- **De minimis (full cash):** where the amount that would otherwise have to be annuitised is small enough, the whole benefit may be taken in cash. Applied **per fund**. Threshold on the total retirement interest: **R247 500** up to 28 Feb 2026; **R360 000** from 1 March 2026. Under the two-pot legislation the same test is expressed as the annuitisable portion (retirement component + two-thirds of the non-vested part of the vested component) not exceeding **R165 000** (= 2/3 × R247 500) — from 1 March 2026 this becomes **R240 000** (= 2/3 × R360 000) ([Helfin](https://helfin.co.za/sars-implements-budget-speech-2026-increases-to-the-de-minimis-thresholds-for-retirement-benefits/), [Sanlam](https://seb-news.sanlam.co.za/consultant-toolkit/key-retirement-fund-values-and-changes-effective-1-march-2026/), [Momentum](https://www.momentum.co.za/momentum/retirement-funds-trustee-newsletter-2025/two-pot-a-year-later)).
- **Living annuity commutation:** a living annuity may be fully commuted to cash if its value (aggregated per insurer) is below **R125 000** (to 28 Feb 2026) / **R150 000** (from 1 March 2026, GG 23 March 2026) ([Sanlam](https://seb-news.sanlam.co.za/consultant-toolkit/key-retirement-fund-values-and-changes-effective-1-march-2026/)).
- Living annuity income must be drawn at between **2.5 % and 17.5 %** of capital per year ([10X](https://www.10x.co.za/living-annuity-faq)).

## 10. PAYE on a GEPF / GPAA pension and the tax directive process

- GEPF pensions are paid by the **Government Pensions Administration Agency (GPAA)**, which withholds **PAYE monthly** using the SARS tables/rebates, exactly like a salary. All benefits accrued after 1 March 1998 are taxable. Pensioners can request in writing that GPAA deduct **additional** PAYE to cover other income ([GEPF FAQ](https://www.gepf.co.za/frequently-asked-questions/)).
- **Multiple income sources / "fixed PAYE rate":** paragraph 2(2B) of the Fourth Schedule (effective 1 March 2022) lets SARS calculate a single effective rate from all of a taxpayer's IRP5/IT3(a) income and issue it to each annuity payer. GPAA implemented these SARS directives from September/October 2022, which is why many GEPF pensioners saw higher deductions. The fixed rate only applies to pensioners with more than one source of taxable income. A pensioner may **opt out** (in writing to GPAA/GEPF) and revert to normal table-based PAYE, accepting the risk of a year-end assessment debt ([GEPF notice 4 Oct 2022](https://www.gepf.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Tax-deductions.pdf), [GEPF reminder](https://www.gepf.co.za/reminder-for-multiple-income-earners-to-make-their-choices/), [SARS income from two sources](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/income-from-two-sources/), [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/how-taxing-annuities-at-the-fixed-paye-rate-will-affect-pensioners/), [Glacier](https://www.glacierinsights.co.za/blog/retirement-insights/sars-tax-directives-2024)).
- **Lump sums (gratuity, resignation, death, two-pot):** the fund administrator must obtain a **SARS tax directive** before paying any lump sum; SARS prescribes the PAYE to withhold (using the tables in §4/§5 and the member's lump-sum history) and the amount is deducted before payment. Employer-paid severance/gratuities use form **IRP3(a)**; fund lump sums use the SARS directive forms (Form A&D retirement/death, Form B withdrawal, Form C RA, Form E annuity events). GPAA cannot obtain a directive if the member's tax affairs are not in order (unregistered, outstanding returns) and outstanding SARS debt can be recovered from the lump sum via an IT88 stop order. SARS typically issues a directive within about two working days; GEPF's service standard is payment within 60 working days of a complete claim (Z102) ([SARS tax directive](https://www.sars.gov.za/individuals/i-need-help-with-my-tax/your-tax-questions-answered/i-want-to-get-a-tax-directive/), [SARS IRP3 guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/completion-guide-for-irp3a-and-irp3s-forms/), [SARS lump sum directive guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/guide-to-complete-the-lump-sum-tax-directive-application-forms/), [GEPF FAQ](https://www.gepf.co.za/frequently-asked-questions/), [MoneyToday](https://moneytoday.co.za/banking/gepf-payment-process)).
- The directive is an estimate; the final liability is settled on assessment (ITR12).

## 11. Estate duty

- Abatement **R3 500 000** per estate (unchanged since 2007); unused portion of a predeceased spouse's abatement rolls over, so a surviving spouse can have up to **R7 000 000** (deaths on/after 1 January 2010) ([Bregmans](https://bregmans.co.za/2026/04/24/estate-duty-in-south-africa-the-complete-guide-for-2025-2026/), [FAnews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/tax/16/tax/1016/estate-duty-abatement-now-portable-between-spouses/7094)).
- Rate **20 %** on dutiable value up to R30 000 000 and **25 %** above (25 % tier from 1 March 2018). Confirmed unchanged in Budget 2026 ([TaxTim](https://www.taxtim.com/za/blog/budget-2026), [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/budget-speech-2026-2027-tax-overview/)).
- **Living annuities and retirement fund death benefits are excluded** from "property" under s3(2)(i) of the Estate Duty Act, so are not dutiable, whether paid to nominated beneficiaries or the estate — but contributions that were not tax-deductible are added back as deemed property (s3(3)(a)bis, from 2016) ([Tax Faculty](https://taxfaculty.ac.za/news/read/estate-duty-living-annuities), [Allan Gray death claim guide](https://www.allangray.co.za/globalassets/documents-repository/product/brochures/Living%20Annuity/Files/Understanding%20the%20death%20claim%20process%20for%20the%20Allan%20Gray%20Living%20Annuity.pdf)).
- If beneficiaries take a living annuity death benefit **in cash**, it is taxed as a retirement fund lump sum **in the hands of the deceased** on the §4 table (sharing the deceased's lifetime R550 000 band); if they continue the annuity, income is taxed at the beneficiary's marginal rate ([Allan Gray](https://www.allangray.co.za/globalassets/documents-repository/product/brochures/Living%20Annuity/Files/Understanding%20the%20death%20claim%20process%20for%20the%20Allan%20Gray%20Living%20Annuity.pdf), [Investonline](https://www.investonline.co.za/insights/leaving-your-living-annuity-to-beneficiaries/)).

## 12. Tax on a living annuity

- Income drawn is taxed as ordinary income at marginal rates via PAYE (subject to the fixed-rate directive regime in §10) ([10X](https://www.10x.co.za/faq/how-is-a-living-annuity-taxed)).
- No income tax, CGT or dividends tax on growth inside the annuity (the underlying fund/insurer policy is exempt) ([10X](https://www.10x.co.za/faq/how-is-a-living-annuity-taxed)).
- Transfer from a retirement fund into a living annuity at retirement, and transfers of a living annuity between insurers, are **tax-free**; SARS still requires a directive and a Recognition of Transfer (ROT01/ROT02) to record the movement ([SARS ROT guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/guide-to-complete-submit-and-cancel-a-recognition-of-transfer/), [10X](https://www.10x.co.za/living-annuity-faq)).
- Section 10C: contributions that were never deducted are exempt when received as annuity income; the payer still withholds full PAYE and SARS applies the exemption on assessment. One adviser source states s10C does not apply to a GEPF pension — unverified ([Biznews](https://www.biznews.com/wealth-advisors/section-10c-income-tax-strategies)).
